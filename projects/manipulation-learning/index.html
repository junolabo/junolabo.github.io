The problem

Most of what the robots do can be written as fixed motions: move to a dispenser, place a cup on a slot. Three tasks could not.

  1. Clearing trash: the object has no fixed shape.A cup always has the same shape, so a rule can grasp it. Trash left on the shelf does not.
  2. Bagging bread: two arms have to work together.Picking up packaged bread and putting it into a bag needs both arms at the same time.
  3. Pouring water for drip coffee: a much more complex motion.Pouring is far more complex than grasping a kettle or placing a filter.

For these, data of a person directly operating the robot was collected, and the motion was learned from it.

How it works

Teleoperation

Ongoing

I implemented the control code and the demonstration-collection code for single-arm and bimanual leader-follower teleoperation. The leader and the follower have the same 6-DOF joint configuration, so the leader's joint values are mapped to the follower's joint commands (joint mapping) inside a control loop.

During a demonstration, camera images, robot state, and actions are recorded and aligned in time, and each demonstration is stored as one episode in HDF5. The dataset is used for policy training.

Manipulation policy (single-arm)

In operation at company-run stores

The beverage-making robot clears trash from the pickup shelf. Only the grasp is learned. The arm first approaches the position found by the ceiling camera. From there, a policy that I trained takes the live gripper-camera image and picks the object up. Dropping it at the disposal position is a fixed motion again.

Manipulation policy (bimanual)

Real-robot testing

The semi-humanoid robot picks up packaged bread with both arms and puts it in a bag. I trained a policy of the Diffusion Policy family. A teammate trained a VLA for the same task. I connected the Task Planning Agent so that it calls this policy and the VLA with an instruction text and an image in which everything except the target is masked.

Drip coffee

Previously in operation

I developed this alone. Drip-coffee making was split into segments, and the learning method was chosen for each segment. Pouring water was a much more complex task than the other two, so it was learned with reinforcement learning.

SegmentMethodHow
Grasping the kettleDiffusion Policy (IL)Learned from teleoperation demonstrations
Placing the filterDiffusion Policy (IL)Learned from teleoperation demonstrations
Pouring waterPPO (RL)Trained in simulation (MuJoCo), then tuned on the real robot

The reward for pouring included posture alignment, smoothness of motion, avoidance of joint limits, and early termination when there was a risk of spilling. On the robot, policy actions at 30 Hz were interpolated and executed as 100 Hz pose commands. After real-robot evaluation, the policies were used in a store service. That service is no longer offered.

Design choices

ChoiceReasonWhat it prevents
Only the part that a fixed motion cannot do is learned.Approach and disposal go to known positions. Only the grasp depends on the shape of the object.Learning a whole task when one part needs it.
A task is split into segments, and each segment gets its own method.Pouring is much more complex than grasping and placing.Forcing one method on every segment.
Image, state, and action are aligned in time when they are recorded.A policy learns which action goes with which observation.Training on pairs that do not belong together.
The planner calls the policy with a masked image and an instruction.The planner has already chosen the target.The policy acting on a different object.
