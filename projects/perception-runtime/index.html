Project 1-1

Perception Runtime

In a store with no staff, the runtime determines the state of the pickup shelf and of every cup from two cameras: which slot is empty, whether the drink came out correctly, and whether the customer took it.

The problem

The robot puts each finished drink on a slot of the pickup shelf, and the customer takes it from there. No person watches the shelf, so every decision about it comes from a ceiling camera above the shelf and a gripper camera at the robot's hand.

  1. A cup that disappeared was not always taken by the customer.The robot also removes cups when it disposes of uncollected drinks. The image alone looks the same in both cases.
  2. Missing depth does not mean an empty slot.When depth drops out or goes stale, the height on a slot is unknown. If unknown is read as empty, a new drink is put on top of a cup that is already there.
  3. Light changes by store and by hour, and the camera looks at glass.Natural light and several kinds of indoor lighting differ from store to store, and the main region the camera looks at is a glass display surface. The camera's own depth was not reliable there.
  4. One arm makes drinks and also removes them.Disposal must not overlap with making, and the cup to remove stands next to cups that belong to other customers.

Structure

How it works

Occupancy detection

In operation

Occupancy is decided from depth. Depth from the trained stereo model and RealSense's own depth are transformed into coordinates based on the shelf plane, and the height of whatever stands on each slot is measured. The depth stream has three states of its own: normal, stale, and bad. While it is not normal, a slot is not reported as empty, and no new drink is placed there.

Earlier version: a classifier on 2.5D features decided the state of each slot. It was replaced by a check on physical height, because the height-based check generalized better.

Cup lifecycle tracking

In operation

Each cup is tracked from making to customer pickup. After making, the gripper camera checks that the cup is held correctly and checks the drink level. On the shelf, the ceiling camera follows the cup. Detection and tracking are implemented in C++ and TensorRT.

The same observation means different things depending on what the robot is doing at that moment.

Slot assignment and the cup record are written together in SQLite, and state is published only after the commit. Other parts of the system therefore never act on a state that was not saved, and the state is restored from SQLite after a restart.

Disposal flow

In operation

The robot picks up and disposes of uncollected drinks, the drinks that customers did not take. This used to be work that a manager travelled to the store for.

Stereo depth

In operation

The decisions above depend on depth, and depth was the weak point: light conditions changed by store and by hour, and the region of interest was a glass surface. So a stereo depth model was trained for this shelf.

Design choices

ChoiceReasonWhat it prevents
“Unknown” is a state of its own for depth.Missing depth looks the same as nothing being there.Placing a drink on a slot that is occupied.
A disappearance is read together with the robot's work state.The image alone cannot tell who removed the cup.Counting a robot removal as a customer pickup.
The gripper camera identifies the cup again before the grasp.A cup found from the ceiling stands next to other cups.Grasping a neighboring cup.
State is published only after it is committed.Other parts act on the published state.Acting on a state that is lost after a restart.
Only one of making and disposal controls the arm at a time.Both jobs use the same arm.Two jobs moving the arm at once.
